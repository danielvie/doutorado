# Saturation stability references for the conditioned cycle map

Date: 2026-09-23. Focus: references that model actuator limits as state-dependent attenuation and prove Lyapunov decrease despite changing attenuation. This note narrows the earlier [saturation review](lyapunov-saturation-reference-review.md) to the current question. It does not change `latex/main.tex` or the bibliography.

The manuscript's object is the **linearized** one-cycle map $e_{k+1}=[A_0+\beta(e_k)(A_1-A_0)]e_k$, with one common $\beta\in[0,1]$ multiplying every switching-instant offset. Its fixed-gain test asks for the same $P\succ0$ to decrease at both endpoints, and therefore at every intervening factor, even if the factor changes each cycle. Source: `latex/main.tex`, Section 4, especially `eq:conditioned-family` and `thm:common-p`.

## Closest verified sources

1. **Hu, Lin, and Chen (2002), strongest theorem analogue.** Their system is $x^+=Ax+B\operatorname{sat}(Fx)$. Lemma 2 puts the saturated input in the convex hull of feedback alternatives; Theorem 1 checks decrease of one quadratic function at the vertex maps to certify a *contractive ellipsoid* for the actual saturated linear plant. For a single scalar input and auxiliary $H=0$, the maps are $A$ and $A+BF$, and standard scalar saturation supplies $\beta(x)=\operatorname{sat}(Fx)/(Fx)\in(0,1]$ when $Fx\ne0$. This is the same two-endpoint algebra as the manuscript's linearized map, but not its coupled timing constraints or exact switched-affine cycle map. The Hu condition also demands that the ellipsoid lie in the auxiliary feedback's admissible region; with $H=0$ this condition is automatic. Primary journal PDF: `research/articles/32-hu-lin-chen-2002-actuator-saturation.pdf`, pp. 99–102, especially Lemma 2, Theorem 1, and its proof. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-6911(01)00168-2>. Already cited in the manuscript.

2. **Gomes da Silva Jr. and Tarbouriech (2001), strongest attenuation-model analogue.** Each input channel has $\operatorname{sat}(F_i x)=\alpha_i(x)F_i x$. On a bounded region, lower bounds on these state-dependent gains give a convex hull of $2^m$ closed-loop matrices; their vertex LMIs and quadratic ellipsoid yield *local* stability. If the channel gains were tied to one scalar, this matrix box would shrink to our segment. Standard componentwise saturation, however, does not preserve the direction of a vector command, and this paper designs the feedback gain rather than merely checking a fixed one. Checked primary conference precursor: [ACC 1998 PDF](http://congres.cran.univ-lorraine.fr/1998/ACC_98/pdffiles/papers/Vol1/WA04_4.pdf), Section 3, equations (7)–(12), and Section 4. Published article: *IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control* 46(1), 119–125, 2001, <https://doi.org/10.1109/9.898703>. Verify final journal pagination/equation numbers if quoting the journal text.

3. **Cao and Lin (2003), alternative if common $P$ is too restrictive.** They describe discrete-time saturated feedback as a state-dependent convex combination of vertex maps, then use $P(\eta)=\sum_i\eta_iP_i$ and conditions involving *both current and next* saturation weights. Setting every $P_i$ equal recovers the common-quadratic case, as the authors explicitly state. Their single-input $H=0$ example writes the map as a scalar combination of $A$ and $A+BF$. This is useful when explaining arbitrary factor changes or possible less-conservative future work, not as a citation for the adopted constant-$P$ computation. Checked primary conference precursor: [CDC 2002 PDF](http://congres.cran.univ-lorraine.fr/2002/CDC_2002/pdffiles/papers/750_FrM05-2.pdf), Sections 2–3, especially equations (4)–(7) and Theorem 1. Published article: *Automatica* 39(7), 1235–1241, 2003, <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0005-1098(03)00072-4>. Verify final journal pagination/equation numbers if quoting the journal text.

## What the search did not establish

Searches also tried "radial saturation," uniform command scaling, and direction-preserving allocation combined with common quadratic Lyapunov tests. They did not uncover a verified source treating **all** of one scalar applied to coupled fixed-period switching-time offsets, a two-endpoint certificate for the resulting cycle map, and stability of the exact nonlinear timing-controlled plant. A radial-pole-path SVSC paper and an adaptive flight-control command-scaling paper appeared, but available publisher abstracts did not verify the same endpoint certificate. Do not promote these to direct precedents without full-text checks.

For the manuscript, Hu is already the closest citation for the stability proof. Gomes da Silva Jr. and Tarbouriech could support a sentence distinguishing common scaling from independent channel saturation in the conditioning section. Cao and Lin could support the remark that parameter-dependent Lyapunov functions may relax a failed common-$P$ test. None turns the manuscript's linearized guarantee into a nonlinear attraction-region result. The general endpoint/common-$P$ trick is prior art; the paper's specific contribution is its dwell-feasible scalar and its use with the one-cycle switching-time model.

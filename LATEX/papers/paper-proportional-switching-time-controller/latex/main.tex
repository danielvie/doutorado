\title[Dwell-conditioned switching-time feedback]{Dwell-Conditioned Cycle-to-Cycle Switching-Time Feedback for Mode-Dependent Switched-Affine Systems}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Daniel} \sur{Vieira}}\email{danielvie@gmail.com}
\author[1]{\fnm{Roberto Kawakami Harrop} \sur{Galv\~ao}}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Systems and Control}, \orgname{Instituto Tecnol\'ogico de Aeron\'autica}, \orgaddress{\city{S\~ao Jos\'e dos Campos}, \state{S\~ao Paulo}, \country{Brazil}}}

\abstract{For a switched-affine plant following a prescribed fixed-period mode sequence, static cycle-to-cycle feedback can locally stabilize a reference periodic trajectory by shifting its $N-1$ interior switching instants, but its raw switching-instant offsets may produce inadmissibly short or negative dwell durations. With strict nominal dwell margin, we multiply the complete raw vector by the largest conditioning factor in $[0,1]$ that keeps every dwell duration admissible; the applied offsets preserve the requested direction, mode order, and cycle period. One scan of the $N$ dwell changes computes the factor in $O(N)$ time, with no online solver. For the linearized raw closed loop, an offline polyhedral iteration finds the maximal subset of the Raw-Action Admissible Region that remains dwell-feasible at every future cycle; Schur stability then gives convergence inside this set. We derive the one-cycle linearization from exact augmented matrix-exponential propagation for mode-dependent affine dynamics. In a nine-interval DC--DC converter benchmark, the computed set has \InvariantRegionFacets{} facets and \InvariantRegionVertices{} vertices and equals the complete Raw-Action Admissible Region. An exact nonlinear \SimulationCycles{}-cycle simulation starts outside this set: the aggressive raw action produces a \MinimumRawDwell{}~\si{\micro\second} dwell, while conditioning enforces the assumed \AppliedScheduleDwellBound{}~\si{\micro\second} bound and the trajectory enters the set at cycle \FirstInvariantRegionCycle{}. The invariant-set guarantee applies to the linearized raw closed loop. The exact nonlinear guarantee is local where conditioning is inactive, and the large-error result is simulation evidence.}

\keywords{switched-affine systems, periodic trajectories, switching-time control, dwell-time conditioning, one-cycle map, DC--DC converter}

\section{Introduction}

Power converters control voltage and current by moving through a sequence of switch configurations. In practice, the switching pattern must respect electrical limits, switching frequency, a minimum dwell duration for each configuration, and a short computation time \cite{karamanakos2020mpcPowerElectronics}. Keeping the individual configurations explicit gives a hybrid-affine or switched-affine model \cite{albea2021hybridAffinePwm}. Repeating the pattern produces a periodic trajectory rather than a constant state, and controlling such trajectories is an established problem for switched-affine systems \cite{egidio2020,xu2024fcsLimitCycle}. We consider regulation at a selected cycle anchor. At each cycle start, the controller steers the state toward that point on the reference periodic trajectory.

Predictive control tackles the problem by solving a constrained optimization problem online. Recent work reduces the online burden through Laguerre parameterization, dedicated fixed-frequency solvers, and candidate pruning \cite{saeed2022laguerre,yang2022fixed,augustine2023mpc}. Controllers also differ in what they may change. State-dependent switching laws choose the active subsystem \cite{egidio2022rank}, where switching-time control keeps an ordered sequence and moves its transition instants. We take this same approach, changing the transition instants and keepping the rest of the parameters the same. At each cycle start, the error is computed and the offsets applied during that cycle, with no online solver.

Patino et al. studied mode-dependent cyclic systems and how each switching instant affects the trajectory. They applied an online predictive controller to adjust the switching instants and track an optimal periodic trajectory \cite{patino2010}. Marcolino et al. considered a fixed-period cycle controlled by changing its interior switching instants. They used a discrete LQR and dwell-constrained predictive control to apply corrections based on the one-cycle model \cite{marcolino2021}. Their model has a common state matrix with switched actuator levels. Each interior switching instant separates two dwell durations, corresponding to the modes active before and after it. Delaying the instant lengthens the preceding dwell and shortens the following one, so the dwell limits are coupled. The predictive controller enforces these limits through online optimization, and its feasible set is characterized through polyhedral projection \cite{marcolino2021}. The standalone LQR comparison instead clips the offsets one at a time, which changes the requested direction and may shift the final scheduled instant \cite{marcolino2021}. Projecting a raw action directly onto the feasible set would require a quadratic program and would generally change its direction. Direction-preserving control allocation scales the complete command, but it also uses online optimization and does not address fixed-period dwell constraints \cite{nakamuraZimmerer2026directionPreserving}. The remaining problem is to find the largest feasible scale directly, preserving the timing-action direction, mode order, and cycle period without an online solver.

We adapt direction-preserving scaling to fixed-period switching schedules. Each dwell constraint gives an upper bound on the scale, and the smallest bound is the largest feasible factor in $[0,1]$. Checking all $N$ bounds takes $O(N)$ time and requires no online solver. Scaling the complete raw offset vector preserves its direction, the mode order, and the cycle period. For the linearized raw closed loop, an offline polyhedral iteration finds the maximal invariant subset of the Raw-Action Admissible Region. Outside this set, the conditioner guarantees current-cycle dwell feasibility but not convergence; the nonlinear guarantee remains local. \Cref{sec:model} derives the cycle model, \cref{sec:conditioning} develops the conditioner and invariant region, and \cref{sec:case-study} presents the converter study.

\section{Fixed-period one-cycle model}
\label{sec:model}

The conditioner of \cref{sec:conditioning} needs two things from the plant: how a switching-instant offset changes the next cycle-start error, and what dwell durations that offset produces. This section derives both, in four steps. We fix the timing coordinates, propagate one cycle exactly in an augmented state, expand that product to first order in the dwell changes, and rewrite the result in the independent switching-instant offsets.

\subsection{Switched-affine cycle and timing coordinates}

Consider
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\vect{x}}(t)=A_{\sigma(t)}\vect{x}(t)+\vect{b}_{\sigma(t)},
    \qquad \vect{x}(t)\in\R^n,
    \label{eq:plant}
\end{equation}
where each cycle follows the fixed mode sequence $(q_1,\ldots,q_N)$ with nominal boundaries
\begin{equation}
    0=\bar t_0<\bar t_1<\cdots<\bar t_N=T,
    \qquad \bar d_i=\bar t_i-\bar t_{i-1}.
\end{equation}
The nominal schedule generates a reference periodic trajectory $\bar{\vect{x}}(t)$. Its cycle anchor $\vect{x}^{\star}=\bar{\vect{x}}(0)=\bar{\vect{x}}(T)$ is a fixed point of the nominal cycle, and we measure the cycle-start error against it as $\vect{e}_k=\vect{x}_k-\vect{x}^{\star}$.

Only the interior switching instants move:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    t_{k,i}&=\bar t_i+\delta\tau_{k,i},\quad i=1,\ldots,N-1,\\
    \delta\tau_{k,0}&=\delta\tau_{k,N}=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The vector $\dtau_k$ contains the $N-1$ independent offsets. The corresponding $N$ dwell changes satisfy
\begin{equation}
    \ddwell_k=D\dtau_k,
    \qquad
    D_{ij}=\begin{cases}
        1,&i=j,\\
        -1,&i=j+1,\\
        0,&\text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:incidence-map}
\end{equation}
for $i=1,\ldots,N$ and $j=1,\ldots,N-1$, so $\delta d_{k,i}=\delta\tau_{k,i}-\delta\tau_{k,i-1}$. Moving one instant later lengthens the interval before it and shortens the interval after it by the same amount. Since $\vect{1}^{\mathsf T}D=0$, the dwell changes sum to zero and every offset vector preserves the cycle period $T$. We write the exact one-cycle map as $\vect{x}_{k+1}=\mathcal P(\vect{x}_k,\dtau_k)$.

\subsection{Exact augmented propagation and prediction}

We suppress the cycle index while deriving the propagation equation. Define the augmented state and matrix
\begin{equation}
    \chi(t)=\begin{bmatrix}\vect{x}(t)\\1\end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    \widetilde A_{\sigma(t)}=
    \begin{bmatrix}
        A_{\sigma(t)}&\vect{b}_{\sigma(t)}\\
        \vect{0}^{\mathsf T}&0
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{\dot\chi(t)=\widetilde A_{\sigma(t)}\chi(t)}.
\end{equation}
For one cycle, let
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    0&=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_N=T,\\
    \sigma(t)&=q_i,\qquad t_{i-1}\le t<t_i.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}
    F_i=\widetilde A_{q_i},
    \qquad
    \Delta t_i=t_i-t_{i-1}.
    \label{eq:augmented-generator}
\end{equation}
Propagation through interval $i$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \chi(t_i)=e^{F_i\Delta t_i}\chi(t_{i-1}).
\end{equation}
Applying this relation from interval $1$ to interval $N$ yields the exact cycle propagation
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
    \chi(t_N)={}&e^{F_N\Delta t_N}
    e^{F_{N-1}\Delta t_{N-1}}\cdots\\
    &e^{F_2\Delta t_2}e^{F_1\Delta t_1}\chi(t_0)
\end{aligned}}.
\label{eq:exact-augmented-cycle}
\end{equation}
The rightmost factor acts first. The matrix order follows the order in which the trajectory visits the modes.

The nominal interval duration and its perturbation are
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\bar t_i=\bar t_i-\bar t_{i-1}=\bar d_i,
    \qquad
    \Delta t_i=\Delta\bar t_i+\delta t_i.
    \label{eq:prediction-duration-perturbation}
\end{equation}
The corresponding nominal cycle is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
    \bar\chi(t_N)={}&e^{F_N\Delta\bar t_N}
    e^{F_{N-1}\Delta\bar t_{N-1}}\cdots\\
    &e^{F_2\Delta\bar t_2}e^{F_1\Delta\bar t_1}\bar\chi(t_0)
\end{aligned}}.
\end{equation}
Define the augmented error by
\begin{equation}
    e(t)=\chi(t)-\bar\chi(t),
    \qquad
    \chi(t_0)=\bar\chi(t_0)+e(t_0).
    \label{eq:augmented-state-error}
\end{equation}
Subtracting the nominal propagation from the actual propagation gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    e(t_N)={}&
    e^{F_N(\Delta\bar t_N+\delta t_N)}\cdots\\
    &e^{F_1(\Delta\bar t_1+\delta t_1)}
    \left(\bar\chi(t_0)+e(t_0)\right)\\
    &-e^{F_N\Delta\bar t_N}\cdots
    e^{F_1\Delta\bar t_1}\bar\chi(t_0).
\end{split}
\label{eq:exact-augmented-error}
\end{equation}

\subsection{First-order prediction in interval durations}

Since $F_i$ is constant on interval $i$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds}e^{F_i s}=F_i e^{F_i s}.
\end{equation}
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
    e^{F_i(\Delta\bar t_i+\delta t_i)}
    =e^{F_i\Delta\bar t_i}
    +F_i e^{F_i\Delta\bar t_i}\,\delta t_i
    +\mathcal O(\delta t_i^2).
    \label{eq:interval-first-order}
\end{equation}
Using
\begin{equation}
    \phi_i=e^{F_i\Delta\bar t_i},
\end{equation}
the ordered product becomes, to first order,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    &e^{F_N(\Delta\bar t_N+\delta t_N)}\cdots
     e^{F_1(\Delta\bar t_1+\delta t_1)}\\
    &\quad\doteq \widetilde\Phi+
    \sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi_N\cdots\phi_{i+1}F_i\\
    &\hspace{7em}{}\times\phi_i\cdots\phi_1\,\delta t_i,\\
    &\qquad \widetilde\Phi=\phi_N\phi_{N-1}\cdots\phi_1.
\end{split}
\label{eq:ordered-product-first-order}
\end{equation}
An empty product is the identity. In particular, the coefficient of $\delta t_1$ begins with $\phi_N\cdots\phi_2F_1\phi_1$, whereas the coefficient of $\delta t_N$ begins with $F_N\phi_N\cdots\phi_1$. Matrices from different modes are not interchanged.

Terms $\delta t_i e(t_0)$ and $\delta t_i\delta t_j$ are second order. After cancelling the nominal propagation in \eqref{eq:exact-augmented-error}, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \delta\boldsymbol{t}
    &=\begin{bmatrix}\delta t_1&\delta t_2&\cdots&\delta t_N\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T},\\
    \gamma_i
    &=\phi_N\cdots\phi_{i+1}F_i\phi_i\cdots\phi_1\bar\chi(t_0),\\
    \Gamma&=\begin{bmatrix}\gamma_1&\gamma_2&\cdots&\gamma_N\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:augmented-dwell-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
Thus the columns of $\Gamma$ follow the same order as the entries of $\delta\boldsymbol{t}$, and the augmented prediction equation is
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{e(t_N)\doteq\widetilde\Phi e(t_0)+\Gamma\,\delta\boldsymbol{t}}.
    \label{eq:augmented-prediction}
\end{equation}
Here $\doteq$ denotes equality to first order about $e(t_0)=0$ and $\delta\boldsymbol{t}=0$.

To recover the physical-state equation, define
\begin{equation}
    \Pi=\begin{bmatrix}I_n&\vect{0}\end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    \iota=\begin{bmatrix}I_n\\\vect{0}^{\mathsf T}\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
At the cycle boundaries, $e(t_0)=\iota\vect{e}_k$ and $\Pi e(t_N)=\vect{e}_{k+1}$. Restoring the cycle index on the duration perturbation gives
\begin{equation}
    \vect{e}_{k+1}\doteq\Phi\vect{e}_k+G_d\delta\boldsymbol{t}_k,
    \label{eq:dwell-linearization}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \Phi&=\Pi\widetilde\Phi\iota
    =e^{A_{q_N}\Delta\bar t_N}\cdots
      e^{A_{q_1}\Delta\bar t_1},\\
    G_d&=\Pi\Gamma.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:physical-prediction-matrices}
\end{equation}
Because every $\phi_i$ is block upper triangular,
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde\Phi=\phi_N\cdots\phi_1
    =\begin{bmatrix}\Phi&\vect{h}\\
    \vect{0}^{\mathsf T}&1\end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eq:augmented-cycle-block-form}
\end{equation}
Thus $\widetilde\Phi$ is the augmented propagation matrix, $\Phi$ is its physical $n\times n$ state-propagation block, and $G_d$ maps the ordered interval-duration perturbations to the terminal physical-state perturbation.

\subsection{Switching-instant offsets}

The duration-perturbation vector in cycle $k$ is $\delta\boldsymbol{t}_k=\ddwell_k$. Substituting $\delta\boldsymbol{t}_k=D\dtau_k$ into \eqref{eq:dwell-linearization} gives the model in the independent timing coordinates
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \vect{e}_{k+1}&=\Phi\vect{e}_k+\Gamma_\tau\dtau_k
    +\mathcal O\!\left(\left\|[\vect{e}_k^{\mathsf T}\ \dtau_k^{\mathsf T}]\right\|^2\right),\\
    \Gamma_\tau&=G_dD.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cycle-model}
\end{equation}
Column $i$ of $D$ increases dwell $i$ and decreases dwell $i+1$. Therefore $\Gamma_{\tau,i}=\Pi(\gamma_i-\gamma_{i+1})$. Since $F_{i+1}$ commutes with $\phi_{i+1}=e^{F_{i+1}\bar d_{i+1}}$, factoring the adjacent terms yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \Gamma_{\tau,i}={}&e^{A_{q_N}\bar d_N}\cdots
    e^{A_{q_{i+1}}\bar d_{i+1}}\\
    &\times\left[(A_{q_i}-A_{q_{i+1}})\bar{\vect{x}}(\bar t_i)
    +\vect{b}_{q_i}-\vect{b}_{q_{i+1}}\right].
\end{split}
\label{eq:timing-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The bracket is the jump in the affine vector field at the switching state, from mode $q_i$ to mode $q_{i+1}$. That is what displacing instant $i$ does. It trades time between two adjacent modes without changing the state at that instant to first order, and the remaining nominal transitions carry the resulting jump to the end of the cycle. \cite{flieller2006limitCycles} also used switching-time sensitivities to compute hybrid limit cycles and test their sampled-time local stability. Equation~\eqref{eq:timing-jacobian} therefore has the switching-sensitivity structure reported in \cite{patino2010}, obtained here from dwell perturbations in independent fixed-period timing coordinates.

If every interval instead shares $A_{q_i}=A_c$ and $\vect{b}_{q_i}=B_c\vect{u}_{i-1}$, then
\begin{equation}
    \Phi=e^{A_cT},\qquad
    \Gamma_{\tau,i}=e^{A_c(T-\bar t_i)}B_c(\vect{u}_{i-1}-\vect{u}_i),
    \label{eq:marcolino-specialization}
\end{equation}
which matches Marcolino's switched-actuator timing model, apart from the sign convention for the input jump \cite{marcolino2021}. When $A_{q_i}$ depends on the mode, the jump also depends on the switching state, so this reduction does not apply in general.

\section{Solver-free dwell-time conditioning}
\label{sec:conditioning}

\cite{briat2013dwellTimes} give stability tests for uncertain linear switched systems under minimum and mode-dependent dwell-time conditions. Here, \cref{sec:model} says what an offset does, not whether it can be applied. A static law knows nothing about the minimum dwell duration, and \eqref{eq:incidence-map} ties each dwell to two adjacent offsets, so clipping the offsets one at a time would change the direction of the correction. We keep the direction and give up magnitude instead, then certify the closed loop that results.

Let a static feedback law produce the raw switching-instant offset
\begin{equation}
    \dtau_k^{\mathrm{raw}}=-K\vect{e}_k,
    \qquad A_{\mathrm{cl}}=\Phi-\Gamma_\tau K.
    \label{eq:raw-law}
\end{equation}
Let $d_{\min}$ be the required minimum dwell duration and assume that the nominal schedule keeps a strict margin above it,
\begin{equation}
    \vect{m}=\bar{\vect{d}}-d_{\min}\vect{1}>\vect{0}.
    \label{eq:strict-margin}
\end{equation}
Each $m_i$ is how much dwell $i$ can be shortened before it becomes inadmissible. The raw action is feasible for exactly those errors in the polyhedron
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal C=\{\vect{e}:\bar{\vect{d}}-DK\vect{e}
    \ge d_{\min}\vect{1}\}.
    \label{eq:admissible-region}
\end{equation}
For $\vect{e}_k\in\mathcal C$ the raw action needs no modification. Outside $\mathcal C$ at least one dwell would fall below $d_{\min}$. Collect the requested dwell changes in $\vect{q}_k=D\dtau_k^{\mathrm{raw}}$ and set
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \beta_k&=\min\left\{1,\ \min_{i:q_{k,i}<0}
    \frac{m_i}{-q_{k,i}}\right\},\\
    \dtau_k^{\mathrm{app}}&=\beta_k\dtau_k^{\mathrm{raw}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:conditioner}
\end{equation}
where a minimum over an empty set is $+\infty$. We call $\beta_k$ the conditioning factor. It equals one whenever the raw action is already feasible, and otherwise the dwell that runs out of margin first decides it.

\begin{proposition}[Feasibility and maximality]
\label{prop:conditioner}
Under \eqref{eq:strict-margin}, \eqref{eq:conditioner} returns the largest $\beta\in[0,1]$ such that every applied dwell duration satisfies $\bar{\vect{d}}+D(\beta\dtau_k^{\mathrm{raw}})\ge d_{\min}\vect{1}$. The conditioned action preserves the cycle period and the direction of the raw action. Computing it takes $O(N)$ time and requires no online solver.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A dwell with $q_{k,i}\ge0$ cannot be shortened by scaling. A dwell with $q_{k,i}<0$ requires $\beta\le m_i/(-q_{k,i})$. Intersecting these bounds with $[0,1]$ gives exactly \eqref{eq:conditioner}, which is therefore both feasible and maximal. The identity $\vect{1}^{\mathsf T}D=0$ preserves $T$, and multiplying the full action by one nonnegative scalar leaves its direction unchanged. A single pass over the $N$ dwell changes evaluates every bound.
\end{proof}

The Raw-Action Admissible Region only guarantees the next schedule. To identify the errors for which the raw law remains feasible at every future cycle, set $\mathcal C_0=\mathcal C$ and iterate
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal C_{j+1}=\mathcal C_j\cap
    \{\vect{e}:A_{\mathrm{cl}}\vect{e}\in\mathcal C_j\},
    \qquad
    \mathcal C_\infty=\bigcap_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal C_j.
    \label{eq:invariant-region}
\end{equation}
This preimage intersection is the standard maximal-admissible-set construction \cite{gilbert1991maximal}. For polyhedral $\mathcal C$, every iterate is polyhedral and redundant inequalities can be removed offline.

\begin{proposition}[Invariant raw-action feasibility and convergence]
\label{prop:invariant-region}
The set $\mathcal C_\infty$ in \eqref{eq:invariant-region} is the maximal positively invariant subset of $\mathcal C$ under $A_{\mathrm{cl}}$. If $A_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is Schur, then every linearized trajectory starting in $\mathcal C_\infty$ remains raw-action feasible, has $\beta_k=1$ for all $k$, and converges to the cycle anchor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Membership in $\mathcal C_\infty$ means $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^j\vect{e}_0\in\mathcal C$ for every $j\ge0$. Thus the raw action satisfies the dwell constraints at every cycle, the conditioner returns one, and $\vect{e}_k=A_{\mathrm{cl}}^k\vect{e}_0$ remains in $\mathcal C_\infty$. No larger subset of $\mathcal C$ has this property by the definition in \eqref{eq:invariant-region}. Since $A_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is Schur, $A_{\mathrm{cl}}^k\vect{e}_0\to\vect{0}$.
\end{proof}

The set computation is offline and does not alter the controller. Outside $\mathcal C_\infty$, \cref{prop:conditioner} still guarantees that the applied schedule satisfies the current cycle's dwell constraints. The conditioned linearized matrix is $(1-\beta_k)\Phi+\beta_k A_{\mathrm{cl}}$, but this set argument makes no convergence claim for trajectories while $\beta_k<1$.

The nonlinear statement is also local. Suppose $\mathcal P$ is continuously differentiable while the mode order is preserved, and that $A_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is Schur. The strict nominal margin and $\dtau^{\mathrm{raw}}(\vect{0})=\vect{0}$ put a neighborhood of the anchor inside $\mathcal C$. Inside that neighborhood the conditioner never acts, $\beta=1$, and the Jacobian of the exact closed-loop map is $A_{\mathrm{cl}}$, so the exact map exponentially stabilizes the cycle anchor and the phase-locked reference periodic trajectory through it. This does not extend the polyhedral certificate to the exact nonlinear map over all of $\mathcal C_\infty$.

\section{Converter case study}
\label{sec:case-study}

The case study asks three questions. Does the derived one-cycle model reproduce the exact plant? What invariant raw-feasible region does the aggressive controller admit? And does conditioning deliver anything that an already-feasible controller cannot?

\subsection{Three-cell converter benchmark and implementation}

The hybrid-affine converter analysis of \cite{albea2021hybridAffinePwm} embeds PWM and sample-and-hold variables and uses exact two-subinterval matrix-exponential propagation in a Lyapunov analysis. The present case study uses a prescribed nine-interval mode sequence. We use the three-cell multilevel DC--DC converter studied by Patino et al. \cite{patino2010}, with state $\vect{x}=[v_{C_1},v_{C_2},i_L]^{\mathsf T}$. For binary switch commands $u_1,u_2,u_3\in\{0,1\}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_{\vect{u}}&=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&0&(u_2-u_1)/C_1\\
0&0&(u_3-u_2)/C_2\\
(u_1-u_2)/L&(u_2-u_3)/L&-R/L
\end{bmatrix},\\
\vect{b}_{\vect{u}}&=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\Eu_3/L\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:converter}
\end{equation}
Both $A_{\vect{u}}$ and $\vect{b}_{\vect{u}}$ depend on the switch state. The plant therefore does not satisfy the common-state-matrix assumption in \eqref{eq:marcolino-specialization}. \Cref{tab:parameters} collects the converter, schedule, and feedback parameters used in this section.

The reported physical mode sequence is $(0,1,3,7,2,0,4,7,4)$, the rounded cycle boundaries are $(0,66,88,110,132,154,220,242,264,286)$~\si{\micro\second}, and the reported cycle anchor is $\vect{x}^{\star}=(9.9247,19.2928,0.9823)^{\mathsf T}$. Because those values are rounded, exact propagation through them does not return to the anchor. We therefore adjust the dwell vector once by least squares, holding the published \SI{\NominalPeriodMicroseconds}{\micro\second} period, the reported anchor, and the \SI{\BenchmarkDwellBound}{\micro\second} nominal-design bound. The resulting schedule is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \bar{\vect{t}}={}&(0,63.889,87.557,109.557,132.538,154.538,\\
    &218.210,240.380,263.828,286)~\si{\micro\second}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:benchmark-schedule}
\end{equation}
The largest boundary correction is \MaximumBoundaryCorrection{}~\si{\micro\second}, and the closure error in the infinity norm is \NominalClosureError{}. The adjustment prepares the benchmark data. It is not a trajectory-design method.

We check the analytical Jacobians against central differences of the exact one-cycle map. The state steps are $10^{-4}$ times the diagonal entries of $S_x$, and the timing step is $10^{-3}t_s=\SI{0.01}{\micro\second}$. A second check applies simultaneous state and timing perturbations with normalized amplitude $\epsilon$ and evaluates
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}

\section{Discussion}

The conditioner keeps the largest feasible scalar multiple of a static timing action and changes neither its direction nor the cycle boundaries. That is also its limit. It cannot look ahead, and it cannot choose a different direction when the requested one is nearly blocked, so it does not replace constrained optimization for controllers that must do either. The invariant region is also computed offline and adds no online work.

The cycle model joins two earlier formulations. Patino et al. derived the mode-dependent switching sensitivity \cite{patino2010}, and Marcolino et al. formulated fixed-period timing coordinates, dwell inequalities, and LQR for switched actuators \cite{marcolino2021}. Our derivation connects the two and checks the result numerically. Generic direction-preserving command scaling is established in control allocation \cite{nakamuraZimmerer2026directionPreserving}. Our control contribution is its closed-form specialization to the coupled dwell inequalities, together with the invariant raw-action feasibility certificate; neither the generic scaling idea nor the LQR design is new here. The certificate is deliberately narrower than arbitrary conditioned stability: outside $\mathcal C_\infty$, feasibility is guaranteed for the applied schedule but convergence is supported only by simulation.

The \SI{\AppliedScheduleDwellBound}{\micro\second} minimum dwell is a simulation assumption rather than a hardware figure. The study leaves out timer quantization, delays, dead time, implementation margin, state estimation, model uncertainty, and experiments, and it fixes the mode order, phase, and period. The exact nonlinear guarantee holds only in a neighborhood where conditioning never acts, so the large-error simulation is evidence for this benchmark and not a global nonlinear result.

\section{Conclusion}

The conditioner enforces every minimum-dwell constraint in $O(N)$ time and keeps both the direction of the raw action and the cycle period. Offline preimage iteration identifies the maximal part of the Raw-Action Admissible Region that stays feasible under the linearized raw closed loop; for the converter design, the complete region is invariant and Schur stability gives convergence inside it. The augmented derivation covers mode-dependent switched-affine dynamics and reduces to the switched-actuator model when the state matrix is common, and finite differences confirm its Jacobians. In the exact nonlinear benchmark simulation, conditioning turns an infeasible aggressive action into an admissible one and the trajectory enters the computed region after three cycles. That entry and the behavior outside the region are simulation evidence. The nonlinear guarantee remains local, and hardware tests are still needed to account for timer quantization, delays, and implementation margin.

# Relation of four saturation and polytopic-stability papers to the manuscript

Status: provisional synthesis
Date: 2026-09-18

## Question

How closely do the four downloaded articles support the modeling and Lyapunov argument in `latex/main.tex`?

The manuscript studies the conditioned linearized cycle map
\[
 e_{k+1}=A(\beta_k)e_k,\qquad
 A(\beta)=A_0+\beta(A_1-A_0),\quad \beta\in[0,1],
\]
where one state-dependent scalar multiplies the complete raw switching-time action. A common matrix \(P\succ0\), checked at the two endpoints, certifies uniform exponential stability for arbitrary sequences \(\{\beta_k\}\). The exact switched-affine plant remains nonlinear in the timing offsets, so the theorem applies to the linearized cycle map.

## Overall assessment

The full texts strengthen the connection considerably for Hu, Lin, and Chen (2002). In the single-gain case with auxiliary feedback set to zero, their saturated closed loop has the same one-dimensional matrix segment used by the manuscript. Their proof also uses the same geometric fact: the next state lies in a convex hull, and a quadratic function evaluated at an interior point does not exceed the largest endpoint value.

The manuscript should therefore avoid presenting endpoint common-\(P\) certification of scalar attenuation as a new general stability technique. Its stronger contribution is the construction of the scalar conditioner for coupled fixed-period dwell constraints, its closed-form maximal factor, and its integration with cycle-to-cycle switching-time feedback for a mode-dependent switched-affine converter.

## Ranked relationship

### 1. Hu, Lin, and Chen (2002): direct theorem analogue

**Reference:** T. Hu, Z. Lin, and B. M. Chen, “Analysis and design for discrete-time linear systems subject to actuator saturation,” *Systems & Control Letters*, 45(2), 97–112, 2002. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-6911(01)00168-2>.

**What the paper proves.** For
\[
 x^+=Ax+B\operatorname{sat}(Fx),
\]
Lemma 2 places the saturated command in the convex hull of linear commands constructed from the requested feedback \(F\) and an auxiliary feedback \(H\). Theorem 1 then checks one quadratic matrix inequality at every selector vertex. If the ellipsoid lies in the region where \(H\) is admissible, the ellipsoid is contractively invariant for the actual nonlinear saturated system. The proof uses convexity of \(V(z)=z^TPz\).

**Exact connection.** For one scalar attenuation channel and \(H=0\), the two vertex matrices are
\[
 A_0=A,\qquad A_1=A+BF.
\]
Standard scalar saturation gives \(\operatorname{sat}(Fx)=\beta(x)Fx\), with \(\beta(x)\in(0,1]\). The closed-loop matrix is therefore
\[
 A+\beta(x)BF=(1-\beta)A_0+\beta A_1.
\]
This is algebraically the manuscript's conditioned family. The manuscript has a vector timing command, but the conditioner multiplies every component by one common scalar, so the induced state matrix still lies on a two-vertex segment rather than in the \(2^m\)-vertex box generated by independent channel saturation.

**Important difference.** Hu et al. certify an invariant ellipsoid for the actual saturated discrete-time system. The manuscript certifies the linearized cycle map. Its exact one-cycle map contains products of matrix exponentials whose durations depend on \(\beta\), so the exact map is not affine in \(\beta\). Hu's nonlinear conclusion cannot be transferred directly.

**Assessment:** very strong match. This is the most important new citation for the common-\(P\) subsection and the geometric proof.

### 2. Gomes da Silva Jr. and Tarbouriech (2001): direct modeling precedent

**Reference:** J. M. Gomes da Silva Jr. and S. Tarbouriech, “Local stabilization of discrete-time linear systems with saturating controls: an LMI-based approach,” *IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control*, 46(1), 119–125, 2001. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1109/9.898703>.

**What the paper proves.** Each saturated input component is written as
\[
 \operatorname{sat}(F_i x)=\alpha_i(x)F_i x,
 \qquad 0<\alpha_i(x)\le 1.
\]
Lower bounds on the \(\alpha_i\) over a prescribed region produce a local polytopic model with \(2^m\) vertices. Proposition IV.1 uses one quadratic ellipsoid and vertex LMIs to guarantee local asymptotic convergence while also imposing unsaturated pole-location requirements. The paper jointly designs the gain and certified region.

**Connection.** This is a direct precedent for interpreting signal conditioning as state-dependent multiplicative attenuation and embedding the resulting closed-loop dynamics in a convex matrix family. If all channel gains are constrained to be equal, \(\operatorname{diag}(\alpha)=\beta I\), its matrix box collapses to the manuscript's line segment.

**Important difference.** Componentwise saturation generally changes the control direction, while the manuscript deliberately preserves direction by applying one scalar to the complete timing vector. The paper also performs local gain synthesis; the manuscript fixes \(K\) before certification and computes \(\beta\) from coupled dwell constraints.

**Assessment:** strong match for the conditioning model and vertex-LMI rationale, but not as exact as the scalar specialization of Hu et al.

### 3. Cao and Lin (2003): less-conservative alternative, not the adopted method

**Reference:** Y.-Y. Cao and Z. Lin, “Stability analysis of discrete-time systems with actuator saturation by a saturation-dependent Lyapunov function,” *Automatica*, 39(7), 1235–1241, 2003. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0005-1098(03)00072-4>.

**What the paper proves.** The saturated closed loop is written as a state-dependent convex combination
\[
 \widehat A(\eta)=\sum_i\eta_i(x)\widehat A_i.
\]
Instead of one common matrix, the paper uses
\[
 V(x)=x^TP(\eta(x))x,
 \qquad P(\eta)=\sum_i\eta_iP_i.
\]
Theorem 1 imposes inequalities for every current and next vertex pair, accounting for changes from \(\eta(k)\) to \(\eta(k+1)\). Setting all \(P_i\) equal recovers the common quadratic method of Hu et al.

**Connection.** The paper directly addresses the issue that the attenuation severity changes from one sample to the next. For the manuscript's two-vertex family, its construction suggests a parameter-dependent candidate
\[
 P(\beta)=(1-\beta)P_0+\beta P_1
\]
and pairwise conditions covering \(\beta_k\) and \(\beta_{k+1}\).

**Important difference.** The manuscript does not use a saturation-dependent Lyapunov function. Its main value here is to document a possible less-conservative extension if a common \(P\) cannot be found. It would still certify the linearized family unless the exact nonlinear cycle map were separately enclosed.

**Assessment:** strong supporting reference for changing attenuation factors and conservatism, but it should not be cited as the method actually implemented.

### 4. Mason, Sigalotti, and Daafouz (2012): general switched-family context

**Reference:** P. Mason, M. Sigalotti, and J. Daafouz, “Comparison between classes of state-quadratic Lyapunov functions for discrete-time linear polytopic and switched systems,” *Systems & Control Letters*, 61(11), 1062–1068, 2012. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sysconle.2012.07.010>.

**What the paper proves.** The paper studies
\[
 x(k+1)=A_{\sigma(k)}x(k)
\]
for finite and polytopic mode sets. It compares static common quadratic, parameter-dependent, parameter-and-time-dependent, and poly-quadratic functions. Proposition 4 gives pairwise finite LMIs for poly-quadratic stability. The paper also studies bounded changes in the scheduling parameter and shows why parameter-dependent tests may be less conservative than a static common quadratic test.

**Connection.** The manuscript deliberately treats the state-dependent \(\beta_k\) as if it could follow any sequence in \([0,1]\). This is an arbitrary-switching or arbitrary-scheduling interpretation of the segment \(\operatorname{co}\{A_0,A_1\}\). A static common \(P\) is sequence independent, which is exactly why it can be carried from one cycle to the next without assumptions on how \(\beta_k\) changes.

**Important difference.** The paper does not study saturation, control constraints, switching-time offsets, or converters. Its main results concern richer parameter-dependent Lyapunov classes, not the elementary endpoint proof used in the manuscript. It is useful for positioning and for explaining conservatism, not as the primary precedent for the conditioner.

**Assessment:** moderate-to-strong framework match. Cite it after introducing arbitrary factor sequences or when discussing possible parameter-dependent relaxations.

## Implications for novelty and claims

### Claims well supported by these papers

1. State-dependent attenuation can be represented as a convex family of linear closed-loop matrices.
2. A common quadratic function checked at the vertices can certify every interior matrix and tolerate arbitrary changes of the convex weights.
3. Common quadratic tests are sufficient and may be conservative compared with parameter-dependent or saturation-dependent Lyapunov functions.
4. Local admissibility or invariant-set conditions must be distinguished from unconstrained global claims.

### Claims that should remain specific to this manuscript

1. The largest feasible scalar for the coupled dwell inequalities has the closed-form minimum-ratio expression in the conditioner.
2. Uniform scaling preserves the complete timing-action direction, the fixed period, and mode order.
3. The switching-time linearization produces the particular segment \(A(\beta)=\Phi_x-\beta\Gamma_\tau K\).
4. The numerical certificate and performance results apply to the stated converter benchmark.

### Claim to avoid

The manuscript should not imply that endpoint common-\(P\) analysis for state-dependent scalar attenuation is itself new. Hu et al. contain a close discrete-time saturation analogue, and general polytopic stability theory already explains the endpoint logic.

## Recommended manuscript placement

- **Introduction or conditioning section:** cite Gomes da Silva Jr. and Tarbouriech (2001) when describing saturation as state-dependent attenuation. Contrast independent componentwise gains with the manuscript's single direction-preserving factor.
- **Beginning of the common-\(P\) subsection:** cite Hu et al. (2002) as the closest convex-hull/common-quadratic precedent. State that the present family has only two vertices because all timing components share one factor.
- **After the theorem or in the SDP limitations paragraph:** cite Cao and Lin (2003) and Mason et al. (2012) for parameter-dependent alternatives and conservatism.
- **Discussion:** state that the novelty lies in deriving the dwell-feasible factor and connecting it to the cycle model, not in proposing a new general Lyapunov theorem.

## Possible positioning paragraph

Discrete-time actuator-saturation analyses often represent the nonlinear feedback as a state-dependent convex combination of linear closed-loop matrices. A common quadratic decrease condition at the resulting vertices then gives a contractive invariant set, while parameter-dependent Lyapunov functions can reduce conservatism. The dwell conditioner considered here has a more restricted structure: one factor scales the complete timing action, so the linearized closed-loop family is the segment joining the zero-action and full-action matrices. The contribution is therefore not a new general polytopic Lyapunov criterion, but the closed-form construction of this factor for coupled fixed-period dwell constraints and its integration with switching-time feedback.

Suggested citations for this paragraph are Hu et al. (2002), Gomes da Silva Jr. and Tarbouriech (2001), Cao and Lin (2003), and Mason et al. (2012).

## Uncertainties

- None of the four papers studies minimum dwell constraints on a fixed-period sequence of switching instants.
- None analyzes the exact nonlinear one-cycle map formed by mode-dependent matrix exponentials with conditioned durations.
- A nonlinear local certificate for the manuscript would require either a bound on the cycle-map remainder or a convex enclosure of the exact map. The downloaded papers do not supply that step.
- The practical value of a parameter-dependent \(P(\beta)\) would depend on the allowed range and transitions of \(\beta\). The present theorem intentionally avoids those assumptions.

## Provisional conclusion

Hu et al. (2002) is a direct mathematical precedent for the endpoint/common-\(P\) logic once scalar attenuation is recognized. Gomes da Silva Jr. and Tarbouriech (2001) supports the state-dependent gain and local-polytope interpretation. Cao and Lin (2003) is best treated as a less-conservative alternative. Mason et al. (2012) supplies the broader arbitrary-switching and parameter-dependent Lyapunov context.

These papers strengthen the manuscript's theoretical positioning but narrow the novelty claim. The defensible novelty remains the solver-free maximal scalar for coupled dwell constraints and its use in cycle-to-cycle switching-time control of a mode-dependent switched-affine system.

1. The augmented state changes the matrix dimension

The physical switched-affine system has state x(t) ∈ ℝn:

x˙(t)=Aσ(t)x(t)+bσ(t).

Appending the constant coordinate 1 gives the augmented state:

χ(t)=[x(t)1]∈ℝn+1.

On interval i, where mode qi is active, the augmented generator is

Fi=A˜qi=[Aqibqi01×n0]∈ℝ(n+1)×(n+1).χ˙(t)=Fiχ(t).

Because Fi is (n+1) × (n+1), every matrix exponential formed from Fi is also (n+1) × (n+1).

2. Why the product of the interval matrices is augmented

The nominal transition through interval i is the exponential of the augmented generator:

φi=eFiΔt¯i=[Ψici01],Ψi=eAqiΔt¯i.

The upper-right vector ci contains the affine contribution accumulated during interval i. Since every φi is augmented, their ordered product is also augmented:

Φ˜=φNφN−1⋯φ2φ1=[Φh01].

The upper-left block is the physical homogeneous propagation:

Φ=ΨNΨN−1⋯Ψ2Ψ1.

Notation only. The reference uses Φ for the complete product φN⋯φ1. The article uses Φ̃ for that same augmented product and reserves bare Φ for its physical n × n block. Φ̃ is not a second propagation process.

3. What the eigenvalue at one means

The augmented one-cycle product represents the affine physical map as an ordinary matrix multiplication:

Φ˜[xk1]=[Φxk+h1].

The periodic cycle anchor x⋆ is a fixed point of this affine map:

Φx⋆+h=x⋆.

After augmentation, the fixed-point equation becomes an eigenvalue equation:

Φ˜[x⋆1]=1·[x⋆1].

This is the eigenvalue at 1. It expresses preservation of the homogeneous coordinate and the existence of the affine fixed point. It does not by itself imply that physical perturbations fail to decay.

The eigenvector associated with 1 is generally [x⋆, 1]T, not [0, 1]T. Its last coordinate is nonzero, so it cannot be an augmented error direction.

4. Why stability uses the physical block

Subtract the nominal augmented state from the actual augmented state:

e(t)=χ(t)−χ¯(t)=[ex(t)0].

The last error coordinate is always zero. Applying the augmented product therefore removes the affine column h:

Φ˜[ex0]=[Φex0].

The timing-sensitivity columns in Γ also have last coordinate zero. Thus the complete augmented error dynamics remain in the invariant subspace ℝn × {0}.

Equivalently, introduce the projection and injection matrices

Π=[In0],ι=[In0],Φ=ΠΦ˜ι.

The Lyapunov analysis should therefore use the physical closed-loop matrix

A(β)=Φ−βGdDK,

not the full augmented product Φ̃. The eigenvalue at 1 lies outside the admissible error subspace and is irrelevant to contraction of physical cycle-start errors.

Answer. The augmented Φ is simply the product of the augmented interval transitions. Its upper-left block Φ governs physical perturbations. Stability is proved for that physical block after closing the timing-feedback loop.

1. The corrected mental model

The proof has two distinct jobs: design a feedback law, then certify what happens when the dwell conditioner scales that law. Keeping those jobs separate removes most of the confusion.

Correction to the three bullets

Your mental modelPrecise version
Find P for β = 1, then find another closed loop for β = 0.Fix A(1)=Acl and A(0)=Φ, then solve one SDP for one P that works for both.
Prove that both endpoints are stable in the same convex space.Prove that both endpoints strictly contract the same P-norm. Individual Schur stability is weaker.
The path is stable because the endpoints share a convex space.The path is stable because a fixed-norm contraction set is convex, and the complete segment lies in that set.

The sentence worth remembering: the proof finds one measuring stick, P, under which both endpoint matrices shrink every vector. Convexity then says every blend of those matrices also shrinks vectors under that same measuring stick.

2. What convexity does and does not prove

“Both endpoints are stable” is not enough. The set of all Schur-stable matrices is not convex, so a segment joining two stable matrices can leave it. The missing ingredient is a common norm.

Laboratory: stable endpoints, unstable midpoint

Move β from zero to one. Both endpoints have spectral radius 0.8, yet the midpoint reaches 1.1. This is the exact failure hidden by the phrase “the endpoints are stable, therefore the path is stable.”

Why are the endpoints stable?

Both endpoint matrices are triangular with diagonal entries 0.8, so both eigenvalues equal 0.8. At β = 0.5, the matrix has diagonal 0.8 and both off-diagonal entries 0.3, giving eigenvalues 1.1 and 0.5.

3. The common-P certificate

For fixed Φ and Acl, the paper searches for one positive-definite matrix P that makes both discrete-time Lyapunov decreases strict.

find P = Pᵀ > 0
such that
    Φᵀ P Φ − P < 0
    Aclᵀ P Acl − P < 0

This is one convex feasibility problem in P because Φ and Acl are already fixed. The implementation adds trace(P)=1 to remove arbitrary scaling and maximizes a common decrease margin t.

Laboratory: watch one common P bound the segment

This laboratory uses illustrative matrices with P=diag(1,4). The solid ellipse is xᵀPx=1; the moving curve is its image under A(β). Change β and compare the actual induced P-norm with the endpoint interpolation bound.

The green image can change shape nonlinearly as β moves, even though the matrix itself changes linearly. The proof does not need the exact green curve. It only needs the straight-line upper bound

‖A(β)‖P ≤ (1−β)‖Φ‖P + β‖Acl‖P < 1.

4. Linearized proof and nonlinear scope

The article makes two stability statements with different scopes. Mixing them would turn a local nonlinear claim into a global one that the proof does not establish.

ClaimWhat proves itRange
Conditioned linearized model is exponentially stable.Common P and the induced-norm triangle inequality.Any state-dependent or time-varying sequence βk ∈ [0,1].
Exact nonlinear periodic trajectory is exponentially stable.Local linearization with Schur Jacobian Acl.A neighborhood where strict nominal dwell margin implies β=1.
Large-error conditioned converter response behaves well.Exact nonlinear simulation.The simulated benchmark and initial condition. This is evidence, not a global proof.
Why does the exact nonlinear proof use β = 1?

The raw timing correction is zero at the periodic anchor. Strict nominal dwell margin then gives an open neighborhood in which the raw correction remains feasible. The conditioner does nothing in that neighborhood, so the exact closed-loop map has Jacobian Acl. Standard discrete-time linearization theory gives local exponential stability because Acl is Schur.

Does pointwise stability for every fixed β prove arbitrary switching?

No. Products of individually stable matrices can grow. The common P is what controls every product: each step reduces the same norm by at least the same factor q, regardless of how β changes.

5. The proof in six steps

Here is the complete linearized proof with each assumption visible.

  1. The applied timing action is the raw action scaled by the conditioner:

    δτapp,k = βk δτraw,k = −βk K ek, with βk ∈ [0,1].

  2. Substitution into the linearized one-cycle model gives

    ek+1 = A(βk)ek, where A(β) = (1−β)Φ + βAcl.

  3. The offline SDP finds one P=Pᵀ>0 and endpoint bounds

    ‖Φ‖P ≤ q0 < 1 and ‖Acl‖P ≤ q1 < 1.

  4. Homogeneity and the triangle inequality give, for every β,

    ‖A(β)‖P ≤ (1−β)q0 + βq1.

  5. Set q=max(q0,q1)<1. Then ‖A(β)‖P≤q uniformly over the complete interval.

  6. Apply that same bound at every cycle:

    ‖ek‖P ≤ qk ‖e0‖P.

    This proves exponential stability for every sequence {βk}, including the state-dependent sequence returned by the dwell conditioner.

Final mental model

The convex optimization is a certificate search, not a path search. It finds one quadratic geometry P for two already-fixed endpoint matrices. Once both endpoints contract that geometry, norm convexity certifies the line segment. The uniform common norm then certifies every product generated as β changes from cycle to cycle.

Diagnostic questions

  • Was K already fixed before solving for P? In the article, yes.
  • Is the β = 0 endpoint another controlled matrix? No. It is the nominal cycle matrix Φ.
  • Would separate Lyapunov matrices for the endpoints be enough? No. The proof needs one common P.
  • Does endpoint Schur stability alone certify the segment? No. The first laboratory gives a counterexample.
  • Does the common-P result prove global stability of the exact nonlinear plant? No. Its arbitrary-β claim applies to the linearized model.

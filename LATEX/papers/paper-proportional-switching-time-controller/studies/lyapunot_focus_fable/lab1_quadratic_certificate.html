Lab 1: hunt a quadratic certificate by hand

System: ẋ = A x with A = [0 1; −2 −3]. Your job: choose P = [p₁₁ p₁₂; p₁₂ p₂₂] so that V = xᵀPx is a valid Lyapunov function. The lab grades both conditions live.

State plane. Orange curves: level sets of your V (ellipses if P ≻ 0). Blue: trajectory from the draggable dot (drag anywhere to move the start). The trajectory should cut every ellipse inward when your P is valid.
V along the trajectory. Valid certificate ⇒ strictly decreasing, always.
Things to try.
1. Start from P = I. Is V = |x|² already a certificate here?
2. Drag p12 negative and watch condition 2 die. The tilt of the ellipse is what the decrease condition polices; positivity of P alone is easy.
3. "A tempting bad P" is genuinely positive definite, a perfectly fine bowl, yet V rises on part of every loop. A bowl is not enough; it must be a bowl the flow agrees to descend.
4. Load the two computed solutions and note they sit at different sliders. The set of valid P is a convex region, not a point: every blend of the two (try averaging the sliders) is also valid.

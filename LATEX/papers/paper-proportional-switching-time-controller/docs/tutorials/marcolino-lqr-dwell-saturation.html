1. What gets conditioned

Marcolino et al. use discrete LQR to compute offsets to the interior switching times of a periodic input. Their LQR gain does not enforce minimum dwell times. In the LQR comparison, the physical actuator postpones a switch if the requested time would leave the previous input level active for less than its required minimum duration.

Let r[0..N] be the nominal switching times, delta[1..N−1] the LQR offsets, and d_min[1..N] the minimum dwell times. LQR requests an interior switch at r[i] + delta[i]. The conditioner produces the actual times actual[0..N]. It changes when the actuator switches, not the sequence of input levels.

The check uses the preceding actual switch. Delaying one switch can force subsequent switches, including the final boundary, to occur later.

Source: Marcolino, Galvão and Kienitz, Predictive Control of Linear Systems with Switched Actuators Subject to Dwell-Time Constraints, Section 5.2, equations (47)–(49) and Algorithm 1. Read the local PDF.

2. The actuator-side rule

Start with the fixed first switch. For each interior switch, choose the later of the LQR-requested time and the earliest time allowed by the previous actual switch. The final boundary has no LQR offset, but may be delayed for the last dwell.

actual[0] = r[0]
for i = 1 to N-1:
    requested = r[i] + delta[i]
    earliest  = actual[i-1] + d_min[i]
    actual[i] = max(requested, earliest)

actual[N] = max(r[N], actual[N-1] + d_min[N])

The actuator then holds the reference input level u_ref[i] over actual[i] ≤ t < actual[i+1]. This is a one-pass schedule repair after the unconstrained LQR calculation. It is not independent clipping of the offsets and is not an optimization over all feasible schedules.

4. What the comparison shows

In the cascading-delay example, offsets [+2, 0, +1, −1, +2] request interior switches at [5, 6, 10, 11, 17] seconds. The requests are ordered, so the red input waveform can be drawn, but its second and fourth dwells are only 1 second. The actuator postpones those switches to 7 and 12 seconds. The last level then needs its 3-second minimum dwell; the cycle ends at 20 s instead of 18 s. The realized boundaries are [0, 5, 7, 10, 12, 17, 20]. The out-of-order preset shows the separate case where the raw requests cannot define a waveform with the prescribed input-level order.

The sequential rule enforces the specified minimum dwells for the scheduled levels, but does not preserve the nominal period when the last dwell needs more time. It also does not give a stability guarantee for the LQR-controlled plant. In Marcolino et al.'s two-disk simulation, Table 4 compares requested and realized dwell times; Figure 7 reports divergence of some state trajectories with this actuator-side conditioning.

The paper's predictive controller handles dwell constraints inside its optimization. Its feasible, fixed-period schedule and the separate actuator-conditioned LQR comparison should not be confused.

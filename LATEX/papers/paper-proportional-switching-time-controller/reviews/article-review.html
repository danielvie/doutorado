Article section 1

Introduction

Four paragraphs, each one making the next necessary, narrowing to the sentence the paper exists to prove.

p. 1 · no equations

The introduction is built as a funnel with exactly four layers. If you can state the fourth, you can state the paper.

The trap it avoids is the survey opening. There is no paragraph on industrial applications, none on switched-affine theory in general, none on periodic control. Every citation is attached to one factual claim and sits immediately after it. Distant literature gets a clause; the two papers the work stands on, Patiño and Marcolino, get most of a paragraph each.

Article section 2

Fixed-period one-cycle model

Builds the two things the conditioner needs: how an offset changes the next cycle-start error, and what dwell durations that offset produces.

pp. 2–4 · eqs (1)–(18) · four subsections

The section opens by saying what it will produce and in how many steps, so the four subsections read as stages rather than as a list of derivations. It is exact through §2.2 and approximate from §2.3 onward, and the paper's entire honesty structure hangs off which is which.

2.1

Switched-affine cycle and timing coordinates

p. 2 · eqs (1)–(4)

Everything downstream inherits these coordinates, so get them straight first. The controller commands offsets, one per interior instant, so there are N−1 of them. The constraint is written in dwell durations, one per interval, so there are N of those. Equation (4) is the bridge, and it is the reason the constraints couple.

2.2

Exact augmented propagation

p. 3 · eqs (5)–(8)

Affine dynamics do not compose as a product of transition matrices, because each interval carries its own forced term. Equation (5) appends a constant coordinate, which absorbs that term and makes the flow on each interval linear. One cycle then becomes an ordered product of matrix exponentials of the actual durations, equation (6).

Nothing is approximated here, and that matters twice over. It is the map the simulation in §4.3 propagates, and it is the object the whole first-order expansion is an expansion of. Equation (8) is just the same product with the nominal cycle subtracted, using periodicity to cancel the anchor.

2.3

First-order expansion in dwell durations

p. 3 · eqs (10)–(14)

Only the interval durations vary in (8), and each enters through a single exponential, so expanding each factor around its nominal duration gives the whole first-order model. Equation (10) is that expansion for one interval; (11) is the ordered product with at most one perturbation kept.

Two things are thrown away, and they are not the same. Terms of order ‖δd‖² are the ordinary truncation. Products δd·e, an error multiplied by a dwell change, are the ones that matter: they are what would couple a large error to a large timing correction, which is exactly the regime the large-error simulation runs in. This is the step the diagram above marks in red.

2.4

Switching-instant offsets

pp. 3–4 · eqs (16)–(18)

Substituting δd = Dδτ back gives equation (16), the model in the coordinates the controller actually commands, with Γτ = G_d D. Equation (17) is the payoff: after factoring the adjacent terms, column i of Γτ is the jump in the affine vector field at the switching state, carried to the end of the cycle by the remaining nominal transitions.

Equation (18) is the sanity check on the whole derivation. Force every interval to share one state matrix and the model collapses to Marcolino's switched-actuator form. The converter does not satisfy that condition, because A_u depends on the switch state, which is the concrete reason this derivation had to be done at all.

Γτ,ᵢ — eq (17)Displacing an instant trades time between two adjacent modes. To first order the state at that instant does not move, so the vector-field jump there is the entire effect. This is the switching-sensitivity structure Patiño reported, reached here from dwell perturbations.
Φ — eq (16)The nominal cycle map, the ordered product of mode transitions. What the error does when the controller stays silent, and later the β = 0 endpoint of the segment in §3.
eq (18)The reduction to prior work, and the reason the general case was necessary.

Article section 3

Solver-free dwell-time conditioning

The contribution. Both propositions live here, and they are proved from different halves of section 2.

p. 4 · eqs (19)–(24) · Propositions 1 and 2

Section 2 says what an offset does, not whether it can be applied. The raw law of equation (19) knows nothing about the minimum dwell, so something must sit between what it asks for and what the converter executes. There are two obvious candidates, and choosing between them is the paper.

Clipping each offset back to its own limit is the reflex, and the resulting schedule is feasible. What it destroys is the relationship to the controller you designed: the applied vector points somewhere you never chose, so the closed loop is no longer A_cl from (19), nor anything you have analyzed. Scaling the whole vector by one number keeps the applied action on the requested ray, which is what makes Proposition 2 possible at all.

Now the payoff, equation (23). Because conditioning only ever scales, the closed-loop matrix cannot be arbitrary. It is pinned to a straight line between two matrices you already know, and a norm ball is convex, so checking the two ends checks the whole line.

Article section 4

Converter case study

Not one experiment but three checks stacked on each other, carrying different weights. Confusing them is the fastest way to overstate the paper.

pp. 5–8 · eqs (25)–(26) · Tables 1–2 · Figs 1–4
4.1

Three-cell converter benchmark and implementation

pp. 5–6 · eqs (25)–(26) · Table 1 · Fig. 1

Equation (25) is the converter, and the sentence after it does real work: both A_u and b_u depend on the switch state, so the plant fails the common-state-matrix condition of (18). That is why the general derivation was needed.

Equation (26) is the reconciled schedule. The published boundaries are rounded to whole microseconds, so exact propagation through them does not close the cycle back onto the anchor. The dwell vector is adjusted once by least squares, holding the 286 µs period, the reported anchor, and the 22 µs design bound. Largest correction 2.111 µs, closure error 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁴. This is data preparation, and the paper says so explicitly.

22 µsPatiño's nominal design constraint on the reference schedule, held fixed during reconciliation. Reported in Table 1 as d_bench.
3 µsA separate assumed bound on what the controller may command, d_min. This is the one that drives β, and the one the limitations call uncalibrated.
4.2

Linearization and certificate checks

p. 7 · Table 2

Two independent checks. The Jacobians are compared against central differences of the exact map, giving relative errors at the level of numerical noise. Then a residual sweep with simultaneous state and timing perturbations decays with slope 2.000, which is the signature a correct first-order model must show; a slope near 1 would mean the Jacobians were wrong.

The certificate follows. An offline SDP returns a common P with ‖Φ‖ = 0.999939 and ‖A_cl‖ = 0.979743, so Proposition 2 certifies the whole family with bound 0.999939. The paper now adds the reconciling sentence: that bound is a worst case over arbitrary conditioning sequences, and once β returns to one the closed loop contracts at ρ(A_cl) = 0.568151.

4.3

Large-error simulation

pp. 7–8 · Figs 2, 3, 4

One hundred cycles of the exact nonlinear map from a normalized initial error of 0.983946, far outside the range used to validate the linearization. Every interval is propagated with exact matrix exponentials; the linearized model is never used here.

The headline sequence: the raw aggressive law asks for a −43.045 µs dwell, which would invert an interval. The conditioner acts on 3 of the 100 cycles, bottoming out at β = 0.293978, and cuts the largest offset from 65.217 to 19.172 µs. The minimum applied dwell is exactly 3.000 µs, which is maximality doing its job. The conservative law never needed conditioning and is the fair opponent; it reaches the 0.01 threshold at cycle 20 against the conditioned law's cycle 6.

The last paragraph exists to pre-empt a misreading. Over 100 cycles the open loop looks divergent in state space, but ρ(Φ) = 0.999850 < 1, so it converges, just slowly enough to stay above threshold until cycle 22 009.

Discussion

Three paragraphs: what the conditioner is and is not, how it sits against prior work, and what the study leaves out.

p. 8 · no equations

Almost every question a referee can ask reduces to "where in the state space are you claiming that?" The paper has three answers and they differ genuinely in strength. This is the single most useful thing to be able to draw from memory.

Article section 6

Conclusion

Restates the two contributions, the supporting derivation and checks, the benchmark outcome, and the two open items.

p. 9 · no equations

If you can reproduce this ladder, you can answer any question about what the paper claims. The tiers are the thing that protects it in review.

Proved
  • Proposition 1: the scan returns the largest feasible β, preserves direction and period, costs O(N).
  • Proposition 2: if one P contracts both endpoints, every A(β) contracts, for every β sequence.
  • Local exponential stability of the exact nonlinear map near the anchor, where β = 1 always.
Verified numerically
  • Analytical Jacobians against central differences, relative error 10−12 to 10−11. §4.2
  • Residual decay of slope 2.000, the signature a correct first-order model must show. §4.2
  • Existence of a common P for this particular design, from an offline SDP. §4.2
Simulation evidence
  • One exact nonlinear run, one initial condition, one benchmark: settling at cycle 6 against cycle 20. §4.3
  • Conditioning turning a −43.045 µs request into an admissible 3.000 µs schedule. §4.3
Assumed
  • A 3 µs minimum dwell, not calibrated to any hardware.
  • Exact plant parameters and a measured cycle-start state, with no delay, quantization or dead time.
  • An open loop that already contracts, since Proposition 2 needs ‖Φ‖P < 1. §3 now states this.

  6. §3 — Why is A(β) a segment rather than an arbitrary matrix, and why does that make the proof three lines long?

  8. §4.3 — Why is the minimum applied dwell exactly 3.000 µs and not 3.4 µs?

Findings: four closed, one blocking, four open

Status verified against the source, not from memory. The four open items are all figure work and all live in the same MATLAB script.

01

Figure 3 showed 25 cycles while its caption claimed 100

Fixed

The plotting script caps the horizon at 25 cycles; the caption and body described a 100-cycle study.

Resolved The caption changed rather than the plot. The 25-cycle window is the right one: the conservative law settles at cycle 20 and panels (c) and (d) are flat after cycle 5.

02

Half the floats were never cited in the text

Fixed

Table 1, Figure 2 and Figure 3 had no in-text citation, which Springer copy-editing flags and which left LaTeX free to strand them on float pages.

Resolved All six labels now have exactly one citation. Figures 2 and 3 also moved from [p] to [tb], which is what actually delivers the placement benefit, and they now sit on page 7 beside their discussion on page 6.

03

The minimum-dwell bound is invisible in both figures meant to demonstrate it

Open

The central empirical claim of §4.3 is that the applied schedule never violates 3 µs and that maximality drives it exactly onto the bound. In Fig. 2(b) the bound is drawn inside a 250 µs range, in Fig. 3(d) inside an 80 µs range. At print size the claim is asserted by the caption and not shown by the plot.

Fix Plot the margin, dwell minus bound, on a log axis, or add a zoomed inset over 0 to 25 µs. The applied curve then sits visibly at zero margin.

04

Figure 4 costs a page and duplicates Figure 3

Open

Panel (b) restates Fig. 3(a) over a longer horizon; panel (a) is a 3D view whose legend covers the axes. The paper is at 10 pages against an 8 to 10 target, and this is the cheapest page to recover.

Fix Drop the 3D panel. Keep one small log-axis plot of open-loop error against cycle number, annotated at the 22 009 crossing, placed beside the paragraph in §4.3 that makes the argument.

05

Red means three different things across the four figures

Open

Red is the vC2 state in Fig. 1, the raw or infeasible action in Figs. 2 and 3, and the open loop in Fig. 4. A reader who has learned that red means "what you must not apply" then meets a red baseline controller.

Fix One encoding for the whole paper: blue for applied, red reserved for raw or infeasible, neutral grey for open loop, per-state colors only in Fig. 1.

06

Figure labels contradict the project's own glossary

Open

"Nominal orbit" is on the avoid list in CONTEXT.md. "Candidate" and "Uniform" appear in three panel titles and two legends but nowhere in the manuscript. "beta" and "us" are ASCII where the text uses β and µs.

Fix Take the label strings from the glossary, and drop the in-figure titles entirely since the captions carry them.

07

The certificate was quoted as if it explained performance

Fixed

A certified bound of 0.999939 sitting near a 6-cycle settling time reads as an admission that the certificate is vacuous.

Resolved §4.2 now says the bound is a worst case over arbitrary conditioning sequences, including the one that never applies the correction, and that the closed loop contracts at ρ(A_cl) = 0.568151 once β returns to one.

08

Proposition 2 needs a stable open loop and did not say so

Fixed

The hypothesis ‖Φ‖P < 1 requires the uncontrolled cycle map to contract, a genuine restriction presented as a neutral technical condition.

Resolved Named in §3 where the proposition is stated, with what is lost without it: the conditioner still runs and still returns an admissible schedule, and only the guarantee disappears.

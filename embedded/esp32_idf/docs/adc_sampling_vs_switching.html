Sampling vs. Switching Instants: Validating and Placing the Control Measurement

  • Technology Stack: ESP32, ESP-IDF 5.5, ADC continuous (DMA) + oneshot, I2S DMA signal engine
  • Key Concepts: switching transients, sampling phase, timestamp uncertainty, guard band, deterministic sampling, near-commensurate clocks
  • Domain: Power-converter closed-loop measurement quality
  • Core Question: Can the control measurement be validated against, or deterministically placed away from, the switching instants?

1. The concern and the verdict

The control loop consumes AN3/AN5/AN6 measurements while the DMA engine is actively switching the power-converter GPIOs. A sample converted near a switching edge is suspect: gate transitions inject transients (ringing, ground bounce, supply dip) that decay over a few microseconds, so a reading taken inside that window measures the disturbance, not the plant. This study analyses two candidate remedies:

  • Scenario A — validate-and-reject: keep the free-running ADC, compute each consumed sample's distance to the nearest switching instant, and invalidate samples that fall inside a guard band around the edges.
  • Scenario B — deterministic placement: sample at a chosen, fixed phase of the switching pattern, at a guaranteed distance from every edge.

Verdict up front

ScenarioVerdictReason (one line)
A — validate-and-reject Not reachable as specified Sample-time uncertainty (~±24 µs) is of the same order as the whole switching cycle (60 µs); the guard test is undecidable.
B — deterministic placement Reachable (and recommended) The trigger ISR already anchors the waveform phase to the CPU cycle counter; a busy-wait-aligned oneshot read on Core 1 places the sample with ~µs-level jitter.

The perhaps counter-intuitive result: the ideal scenario is the feasible one, and the fallback scenario is the one physics rules out. The reason is that Scenario A needs information the acquisition pipeline destroys (exact conversion times), while Scenario B only needs information the signal engine already produces (the exact waveform phase, captured at every control trigger).

2. The six timing facts that decide the question

Everything below follows from six measured facts of the current system (alpha = 0.5 dataset, DMA engine):

#FactValue
1Switching pattern cycle (repeats 4× per DMA pass)60 µs, edges at dataset step boundaries
2ADC conversion cadence (continuous mode, 250 kS/s over 3 channels)one AN3/AN5/AN6 triple every 12 µs; one DMA frame (4 triples) every 48 µs
3ADC and signal clocksindependent dividers — the ADC is free-running with no phase relation to the switching edges
4Sample timestamps (current pipeline)stamped at read time; the newest frame finished anywhere in the preceding 0–48 µs, unknowable per drain
5Waveform phase anchorthe control-trigger ISR captures esp_cpu_get_cycle_count() at a fixed, known position of the waveform — sub-µs precision, already implemented
6Post-edge disturbance window (ringing/settling)a few µs; guard band assumed ~5 µs in this study (to be measured on the converter)

Fact 3 plus the near-commensurate cadence (12 µs divides 60 µs exactly) is the under-appreciated danger: the sampling phase walks slowly through the cycle. The system does not get independent random phases each cycle — it gets long dwell periods, including dwell periods inside the disturbance bands.

3. Scenario A: validate-and-reject - why it fails

Scenario A keeps the free-running ADC and adds a time-domain gate: for each consumed triple, compute the distance from its conversion instant to the nearest switching edge, and invalidate it if the distance is below the guard band (~5 µs). The edge positions are known perfectly — the firmware renders them. The question is whether the sample's conversion instant is known well enough to run the comparison.

The uncertainty budget says no

Timestamp error sourceMagnitude
Newest frame's completion vs. read instant (read-time stamping; the frame finished somewhere in the previous frame period)0–48 µs, unknowable per drain
Drain wake latency after the trigger ISRa few µs
Backdating model error (nominal vs. actual ADC clock)<1 µs over a frame
Channel mux skew inside a triple (3 sequential conversions)~4 µs per channel, known

The first row dominates: the consumed sample's true conversion time is known only to about ±24 µs — on a switching cycle that is 60 µs long with a required decision resolution of ~5 µs.

Why the missing information cannot be recovered cheaply

Accurate per-frame conversion timestamps are exactly what the deleted ISR timestamp ring tried to provide — and the control-latency study showed it is structurally incompatible with flush_pool: the driver silently discards frames, orphaning their timestamps and desynchronising the pairing. Restoring honest timestamps means disabling flush_pool, which brings back the backlog/staleness problem that study solved. Scenario A sits on a circular trade-off: it needs timing fidelity the low-latency pipeline had to give up.

Verdict

Not reachable as specified. A weakened variant — value-domain plausibility filtering (rate-of-change limits, outlier rejection against the previous accepted triple) — remains available and is worth having as a defensive layer, but it detects consequences of a bad sample, not its timing, and offers no guarantee.

4. Scenario B: deterministic placement - why it works

Scenario B flips the dependency around. Instead of asking "when was this sample converted?" (unknowable, Scenario A), it asks "can we convert at a chosen instant?" — and every ingredient for that already exists in the firmware:

  1. Phase anchor (already implemented). The control-trigger ISR captures esp_cpu_get_cycle_count() at a fixed position of the waveform (s_trigger_cycles). The waveform phase is thereby known in CPU cycles with sub-µs precision, at every pass.
  2. Deterministic wait (already exists as a primitive). The control task computes target = s_trigger_cycles + offset_cycles and busy-waits on the cycle counter — the same wait_until_cycle technique the CPU engine uses for its edges. Alignment error: well under 1 µs.
  3. Lock-free conversion at the target. Read AN3/AN5/AN6 via adc_oneshot_read_isr() (the IRAM-safe, mutex-free oneshot API), giving three conversions at deterministic instants a few µs apart.
  4. Publish as today. The triple goes into the same seqlock; telemetry, the correction math, and the debug-read path are untouched.

Jitter budget

SourceMagnitudeCharacter
Trigger ISR cycle-count capture<1 µsconstant-ish, calibratable
Busy-wait exit precision<0.1 µsnegligible
Conversion aperture (sample instant at conversion start)<1 µsdeterministic
I2S ISR preempting the wait/conversion (interrupts are on)a few µsrare, and detectable — see below

Expected placement accuracy: ~±1–2 µs typical against a 5 µs guard requirement. And the one non-deterministic term is self-auditing: after the conversion train, the task re-reads the cycle counter; if the train overran its window (an ISR landed mid-sequence), that triple is invalidated. This is Scenario A's validity check — suddenly decidable, because the timing is measured directly in cycle counts instead of reconstructed from pipeline timestamps. B does not just replace A; it makes A's check finally work.

Costs and consequences

  • Driver ownership flips while running. Oneshot and continuous cannot own ADC1 simultaneously, so while the DMA engine runs the ADC operates in oneshot mode owned by Core 1, replacing the inline continuous drain. The reader-token hand-off machinery built for the drain is reused as-is; continuous mode remains for the stopped state and the CPU engine.
  • Rate telemetry changes meaning, not substance. One triple per control trigger (~4.2 k/s) — exactly what control consumes today; the 250 kS/s background stream disappears while running (it was already discarded down to the newest triple).
  • The sampling offset is a dataset property. Step layouts differ per dataset, so the quiet window does too. The render path already knows every edge; it can auto-select the midpoint of the longest step and reject datasets whose longest quiet window is narrower than 2×guard + conversion train (~18 µs) — turning "no safe sampling point exists" into an explicit, visible error instead of silent bad data.
  • Budget. Wait (≤60 µs idle) + 3 conversions (~12–30 µs) + existing math, vs. the measured 140 µs drain it replaces — roughly budget-neutral; add a sample_window_us metric like drain_max_us to keep it audited.

Verdict

Reachable. All primitives exist; the work is composition plus validation.

5. Recommendation and phased plan

Recommendation

Adopt Scenario B (deterministic placement), implemented as sample-at-offset in the Core-1 control task, with the overrun self-check that gives Scenario A's validation for free. Make the offset a dataset-derived property (midpoint of the longest quiet interval, auto-computed at render time), and make "no safe window exists" an explicit dataset rejection rather than silent degradation.

Phased plan (each step independently useful)

  1. Fix the sense inputs first. AN3/AN5/AN6 currently read raw 0, so no disturbance is observable at all. Until real voltages arrive, this whole study is about timing plumbing, not signal quality.
  2. Measure the real guard band. Scope the AN inputs around switching edges on the actual converter; replace the assumed 5 µs with the measured settling time. Every design number downstream depends on it.
  3. Instrument phase before changing anything. One cheap addition to the current pipeline: record (read_cycles − s_trigger_cycles) for each consumed triple and expose min/max/histogram over the UART agent. This measures the actual phase distribution and dwell behaviour of today's free-running sampling — the "before" evidence.
  4. Implement deterministic sampling behind the existing reader-token hand-off (oneshot owned by Core 1 while running), with the overrun check invalidating any triple whose conversion train was preempted. Add sample_window_us and placement_jitter_max to the status struct and UART JSON.
  5. Validate on the harness: placement jitter ≤ 2 µs, overrun invalidations ≈ 0, age_used_max_us unchanged or better, and the existing pass criteria still green.

If B ever proves unworkable: the fallback ladder

Scenario B's residual risk is oneshot driver overhead in the maintenance window. Should it fail on hardware, the best-achievable alternatives, in order:

  1. Median-of-drained-triples. Keep the continuous inline drain, but instead of consuming only the newest triple, take the median of the ~5 triples drained per trigger. Edge-corrupted samples are outliers; the median rejects up to two of five without any timing knowledge. Cost: a few µs of sorting; no driver changes.
  2. Value-domain plausibility gate. Reject triples whose jump from the last accepted value exceeds a physical rate limit. Catches gross corruption only.
  3. Accept and document the free-running behaviour, using the phase instrumentation of step 3 to at least flag dwell periods when sampling phase statistically overlaps guard bands (frame-level, coarse, advisory-only).
